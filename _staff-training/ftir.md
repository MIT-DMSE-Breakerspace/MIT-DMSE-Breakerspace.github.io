# Nicolet iS5 FTIR Staff Training Guide

This pilot guide standardizes a one-hour general FTIR training using the routine iD7 ATR workflow. It draws its operating content from the [canonical instrument page](../instruments/ftir.md), including the two-page Quick Guide on that page. Do not maintain a separate operating sequence here.

## Training Outcome

By the end of the session, participants should be able to:

* Explain what an FTIR spectrum represents and why ATR contact and crystal cleanliness matter.
* Decide whether a routine sample is compatible and recognize samples that require staff review.
* Start OMNIC, verify the instrument connection, clean the ATR crystal, and collect a background.
* Select an appropriate routine ATR loading approach for a flat solid or powder.
* Preview, collect, name, and individually save native spectra.
* Recognize weak contact, a contaminated background, and other stop-and-ask conditions.
* Unload the sample, clean and cover the crystal, close OMNIC, log out, and leave the FTIR powered on.
* Find the Quick Guide, detailed operating instructions, common failure modes, reservations, and help pathways.

The session does not currently include a separate individual skills demonstration. Participants may divide the workflow, but the trainer must make the complete background/sample/save/clean cycle visible and involve every participant meaningfully.

## Level 1 Exercise: Compare Two Known Solids

### Question Or Goal

How do spectra from two known materials differ, and what practical signs show whether each ATR measurement was collected cleanly and with adequate sample contact?

### Sample And Preparation

Use an approved known polymer with a smooth contact face and an approved paper product. Before adoption, record each sample’s identifier, expected major spectral features, cabinet location, and replacement plan in Guide Control or the linked sample-library records.

The legacy 3.000 coffee-roast exercise is a useful course-specific variation but should describe its output as a relative O-H-associated peak ratio—not percent water or direct moisture content—unless a calibrated quantitative method is developed and validated.

### Participant Workflow

1. Inspect the crystal and samples; identify compatibility or contamination concerns.
2. Start OMNIC, confirm the connection, clean the crystal, and collect a clean background using the Quick Guide.
3. Load the known polymer using the self-leveling tip and tighten until the clutch slips.
4. Remove gloves, preview the spectrum, and decide whether contact is adequate.
5. Collect the spectrum with a descriptive name and save the native spectrum file.
6. Wear gloves, unload the polymer, and clean the crystal and plate completely.
7. Load, preview, collect, name, and save the paper-product spectrum.
8. Compare several major bands and the overall patterns. Do not assign a material from one peak alone.
9. Identify evidence of poor contact, a dirty background, water vapor, carbon dioxide, solvent, or residue if present.
10. Unload the sample, clean and cover the crystal, close OMNIC, and log out using the Quick Guide.

### Suggested Roles For Three Participants

* **Sample and cleaning lead:** handles approved samples, pressure tower, crystal cleaning, loading, and unloading while gloved.
* **Instrument lead:** confirms connection, collects the background, previews contact, and acquires spectra with gloves removed.
* **Data and quality lead:** names and saves each spectrum, compares the result with the reference, and identifies quality concerns.

Rotate roles between the two materials. Every participant should complete or narrate at least one glove transition and one save/quality check.

### Success Criteria

* [ ] A clean background and two recognizable spectra were collected.
* [ ] Both native spectrum files were individually selected, named, and saved in the intended location.
* [ ] Participants can explain why ATR contact matters and identify a weak-contact spectrum.
* [ ] Participants compare several bands or the overall pattern rather than assigning from one peak.
* [ ] Participants can identify crystal/background contamination cues and know when to recollect the background.
* [ ] Participants can find the Quick Guide, detailed instructions, common failure modes, reservations, and help pathways.
* [ ] The crystal is clean, covered, and clamped; OMNIC is closed; Windows is logged out; and the FTIR remains powered on.

### Stop And Ask For Help

* The sample is sharp, sticky, abrasive, very hard, reactive, odorous, unknown, staining, difficult to clean, or otherwise outside the approved routine workflow.
* The crystal is scratched, chipped, stained, visibly contaminated, or cannot be cleaned with the routine method.
* The accessory or pressure-tip choice is unclear.
* The instrument will not connect or OMNIC reports an unfamiliar error.
* The background remains abnormal after cleaning and recollection.
* The spectrum remains weak or unusual after correcting obvious contact and contamination problems.

## Trainer Notes And Variations

* For project samples, complete the known-sample cycle first unless the project sample was approved and is equally safe and predictable.
* A non-volatile liquid is a useful extension after the solid workflow is consistent, but it should not displace the full background/save/clean sequence.
* The 3.000 coffee exercise can be offered as a guided comparison after its quantitative language and expected result are revised.
* Treat a database match as evidence rather than proof, particularly for mixtures or contaminated samples.
* Transmission and diffuse-reflectance accessories are outside routine Level 1 training unless the session was specifically approved for them.

## Guide Maintenance

When this guide changes, review the public Quick Guide and operating page, approved sample kit, reference spectra, sample-library records, LibCal description, and Moira information together. Practice the guide with a staff or student-staff trainer before marking it ready for routine delivery.
