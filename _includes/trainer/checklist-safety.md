Shared safety-orientation checklist, delivered at the start of every instrument
training. The canonical explanation of why each item exists is in
_staff-training/lab-safety-orientation.md; this file is only the checkable steps.

Included inline in each instrument guide so the trainer works one continuous
checklist. Edit here once rather than per guide.
{%- endcomment -%}
* [ ] Walk the exit route, or point along its full length. Say that the lab has **one** route: lab door → lounge → Building 8 corridor → nearest building exit. No elevators.
* [ ] Point out the sink-area emergency equipment: first-aid cabinet above the sink, spill kit, and the emergency shower and eyewash immediately left of the sink. Say that eyes or skin means **fifteen minutes** of flushing, not a few seconds.
* [ ] Point out the sharps container near sample preparation, and say **glass slides go in it**, not the regular trash.
* [ ] Show the campus phone on the desk by the lab doors. Dial **100** from it, or **617-253-1212** from a mobile. Say to use these rather than 911 on campus.
* [ ] Have participants say the room aloud: **Building 8, Room 8-102A**, entered through the Breakerspace Lounge.
* [ ] Make the fire distinction explicit: the campus phone is *inside* the lab, so for fire or smoke, leave the building and call from outside instead.
* [ ] Say that **fire alarm pulls are in the main corridor, never inside the lab** — you pass one on the way out. Point out the extinguisher left of the sink and state that MIT policy is not to fight a fire yourself.
* [ ] Point at the food and drink boundary: fine in the lounge, not in the lab.
* [ ] Demonstrate the glove transition rather than describing it: gloves on for sample handling, **off before keyboard, mouse, or touchscreen**, clean gloves back on to return to the sample.
* [ ] Close with the culture point: find staff in the lab, otherwise `#breakerspace-help` or the staff email. **Stop rather than push through** anything unfamiliar, and report damage, spills, injuries, and near misses even when they seem resolved.
